Research

My master's thesis and the papers that came out of it. The full thesis is under embargo until May 2027, so this summary sticks to the public abstract.

Master's thesis · San José State University · 2026

Targeted Bottlenecking of Self-Supervised Speech Layers for Generalizable Audio Deepfake Detection

Advisor: Dr. Faranak Abri · Committee: Dr. Amith Kamath Belman and Dr. Saptarshi Sengupta

Defended May 2026 Full text under embargo until May 2027 Abstract on ScholarWorks (DOI)

The problem

A deepfake detector trained on one collection of recordings often fails on audio that was captured differently: a different recording channel, a compressed audio codec, or a voice synthesizer it has not seen before.

The question

Self-supervised speech models process audio through a stack of layers. The thesis asks where in that stack the unwanted channel and codec information is concentrated, and whether removing it there helps a detector carry over to new data.

The approach

GLADA combines the model's layers in groups. Each group gets a variational information bottleneck, which limits how much detail passes through, and an adversarial domain discriminator, which pushes the features to look the same across datasets. A CKA similarity penalty keeps the groups from learning redundant features.

What it found

Across multiple training seeds and five test sets (In-the-Wild, ASVspoof 2021 LA and DF, WaveFake, and CodecFake+), using the bottleneck only on the lowest layer group beat both a single bottleneck after pooling and a bottleneck on every group, on every secondary out-of-domain test set. Linear probes on each group and CKA comparisons with handcrafted acoustic measurements both trace codec and channel information to that lowest group, which supports putting the bottleneck there.
